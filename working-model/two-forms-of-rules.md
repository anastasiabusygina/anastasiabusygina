# Two forms of rules

This document is a companion to *Working Model: Owner ↔ Contributor*. It addresses one observation that does not fit inside a specification: rules can exist in two forms, and the absence of both produces a specific structural failure.

## Two forms

Rules in a working engagement exist in one of two forms.

**Internalized.** Each participant acts according to the rules without consulting an external document. Responding within a business day, recording decisions, raising disagreement explicitly, providing means together with obligations — all of these happen by default, not because they are written somewhere. The rules are present in the work itself, not in a separate artifact.

**Explicit.** The rules exist as a written document that participants refer to. Behavior conforms to the document because the document exists and is treated as binding.

Both forms produce the same outcome: the deterministic process described in the working model. The mechanism differs; the result is the same.

An engagement can operate in either form. It can also operate in a mix — some rules internalized, others explicit.

## Absence of both forms

When neither form is present — when rules are not internalized and not written down — the same structural failure occurs. It takes the form described in rule 2 of the working model: authority and responsibility separate.

Concretely: one participant makes decisions without bearing their consequences; the other is responsible for outcomes without the ability to influence the decision. The first holds authority without risk. The second holds risk without authority.

This is observable independent of how the participants describe their working relationship. It occurs in engagements that describe themselves as formal and in engagements that describe themselves as friendly. The descriptor is irrelevant to the structural fact: where rules are absent in both forms, this divergence appears.

## A note on translation

Engagements that operate by internalized rules are sometimes described in non-systemic language — as "friendly" or "trust-based." This description is accurate when rules are present in the internalized form: behavior in such an engagement matches what the working model specifies.

The same description is sometimes applied to engagements in which rules are absent in both forms. In such cases the description does not match the structure: what is observed is not internalized rules but their absence, and the divergence of authority and responsibility follows. The engagement is then operating in a configuration the working model would identify as unstable, regardless of how the participants name it.

This document does not propose preferring one form of rules over the other. Internalized and explicit rules are equivalent at the level of what they produce. The document only notes that a name does not substitute for either form, and that observing which form is actually present requires looking at behavior rather than at description.

## Scope

This document describes a structural observation that follows from the working model but is not part of its specification. Its purpose is to make explicit what the specification leaves implicit: that rules can exist in more than one form, and that the absence of all forms has a predictable signature.
